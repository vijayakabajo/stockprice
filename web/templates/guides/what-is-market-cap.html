What is market capitalization?

Market capitalization, or market cap, is the total market value of a company's shares: the share price multiplied by the number of shares outstanding.

Market cap measures what the stock market values a company's equity at today. The SEC's investor education site, Investor.gov, defines it the same way, as share price times total outstanding shares [1].

How do you calculate market cap?

Multiply the current share price by the number of shares outstanding.

Market cap = share price × shares outstanding. A company with 100 million shares trading at $50 has a market cap of $5 billion.

Shares outstanding come from a company's SEC filings, such as the cover page of its 10-K or 10-Q [2]. When a company has several share classes, its total market cap adds up every class.

What are mega, large, mid, small, micro and nano caps?

They are size classes by market cap: mega caps are worth $200 billion or more and nano caps less than $50 million, with four classes in between.

The cut-offs are conventions, not regulations, and data providers draw them slightly differently. This site uses the thresholds below on every company profile. Company counts are live, as of {{ o.as_of|asof }}.

Why does market cap change every day?

Market cap changes whenever the share price changes, so it moves every trading day; it also shifts when a company issues or buys back shares.

That is why every market cap on this site carries an "as of" date. A market cap without a date cannot tell you whether it reflects today's price or last quarter's.

What does market cap not tell you?

Market cap values only a company's equity: it ignores debt and cash, and says nothing about profits.

  • Debt and cash. Enterprise value adds debt and subtracts cash, and is closer to the price of buying the whole business.
  • Profitability. Two companies with the same market cap can earn very different profits; compare revenue and earnings in their 10-K filings.
  • Liquidity. A large market cap does not guarantee that the shares trade often or in volume.
